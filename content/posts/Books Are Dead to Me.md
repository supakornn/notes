I grew up learning from books. When I was young, I enjoyed reading programming books more than anything else. I read books about Python, and many other programming topics. I probably read more than ten books just to understand the programming world. That was how I started. Most of my fundamentals came from books, and I am still grateful for that.

For me, reading was never only about learning syntax or understanding what a concept does. I always wanted to know the story behind it. Why did Uncle Bob introduce SOLID? What problems did developers have at that time? Why was a specific pattern or principle needed? I did not want to just know what something is. I wanted to understand why it was invented and what problem people were trying to solve.

Sometimes I spent three to five days reading one book in my free time. I liked taking my time with it. I liked thinking about the author’s point of view and trying to understand the world that made those ideas necessary. Books helped me build the habit of learning deeply, not just collecting information.

But lately, I feel that the way I learn has changed. I do not think books are always the best way for me to learn anymore. That does not mean books are not great. I still believe books are one of the best ways to go deep into a topic. But AI has changed the way I can explore ideas.

Before AI, if I had a question while reading, I might need to search through another book, articles, forums, or documentation. Now, I often learn with AI instead of starting with a book. I can ask AI to teach me a concept from the beginning, summarize the important parts, and walk through it with me step by step. If I do not understand something, I can stop and ask more questions immediately. I can ask who invented the idea, why we need it, what problem people were trying to solve, what existed before it, and what trade-offs came with it. It feels more like having a teacher I can talk to than reading alone.

I still have dreams to chase. I need to graduate, find a job, and build my future. I cannot spend several days reading every book like I used to. So now I use AI to learn many concepts. It helps me move faster, but I try not to let it make me lazy.

I always remind myself: do not become a [meat proxy](https://dontbeameatproxy.com/). Do not ask AI, “What is this?”, read one answer, copy it, and move on. That is not learning. If I really want to understand something, I need to keep asking questions. I need to learn about its history, the people behind it, the problems they faced, and why their solution made sense at that time.

The tool has changed, but the curiosity should stay the same. Books taught me how to think deeply. AI helps me explore faster. I do not want AI to replace [[Thinking With AI|my thinking]]. I want it to help me ask better questions.

I still believe we need [[Engineering Taste]] to judge what AI gives us. AI can explain things, write code, and give us many answers, but we still need to know when an answer is good, when it is wrong, and when it does not fit the problem.

I do not think LLMs will replace human brains. I think the real risk is that we replace our own brains with LLMs. If we stop thinking, stop questioning, and only accept whatever AI gives us, then we lose the most important part of learning. When you can feel the problem that made someone invent an idea, you do not just memorize the concept. You understand the world a little better.
