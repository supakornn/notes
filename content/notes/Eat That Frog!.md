Book written by **Brian Tracy**.

> “The frog” is your hardest, most important task. Do it first before everything else.

These are the 21 rules from _Eat That Frog!_ that can dramatically improve productivity, discipline, and focus.

1. **Set the table** — Be completely clear about what you want to achieve. Unclear goals create hesitation and procrastination.
    
2. **Plan every day in advance** — Decide what you will do before the day starts. A written plan reduces wasted time and mental friction.
    
3. **Apply the 80/20 Rule** — A small number of tasks usually create most of the results. Focus on high-impact work instead of staying “busy.”
    
4. **Consider the consequences** — Important tasks usually have long-term benefits. Prioritize work that meaningfully improves your future.
    
5. **Practice creative procrastination** — Since you cannot do everything, intentionally delay or ignore low-value tasks.
    
6. **Use the ABCDE Method** — Categorize tasks by importance and always complete “A” tasks before touching less important work.
    
7. **Focus on key result areas** — Identify the responsibilities that truly define success in your role or career.
    
8. **Apply the Law of Three** — Usually, three tasks contribute the majority of your results. Identify and prioritize them daily.
    
9. **Prepare thoroughly before you begin** — Gather tools, information, and resources beforehand to reduce resistance to starting.
    
10. **Take it one oil barrel at a time** — Large goals become manageable when broken into smaller actionable steps.
    
11. **Upgrade your key skills** — Improving your most valuable skills increases confidence, speed, and effectiveness.
    
12. **Identify your key constraints** — Find the bottleneck slowing your progress and focus on fixing it first.
    
13. **Put pressure on yourself** — Develop self-discipline by acting as if deadlines already exist, even without external pressure.
    
14. **Maximize your personal powers** — Productivity depends heavily on energy, sleep, health, and mental clarity.
    
15. **Motivate yourself into action** — Maintain a constructive mindset and focus on progress instead of excuses or negativity.
    
16. **Practice creative procrastination with technology** — Avoid distractions such as notifications, endless scrolling, and unnecessary context switching.
    
17. **Do the most difficult task first** — Tackle the hardest and most important task while your mental energy is highest.
    
18. **Slice and dice the task** — Reduce overwhelm by starting with a very small part of the task.
    
19. **Create large chunks of time** — Deep work requires uninterrupted focus. Protect long periods of concentration.
    
20. **Develop a sense of urgency** — High performers act decisively and avoid unnecessary delays.
    
21. **Single-handle every task** — Once you start an important task, continue working on it until it is meaningfully completed.
