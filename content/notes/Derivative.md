The formal **definition of a derivative** is built on a [[Limit|limit]]. A speedometer or slope analogy helps visualize it, but the definition lets us calculate a slope at one point, where ordinary rise over run fails because the run is zero.

### Formal Definition
The derivative of a function $f(x)$ , denoted as $f'(x)$ or $\frac{dy}{dx}$ , is defined by the limit:


$$
f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}
$$


### Geometric Interpretation
On a graph, the derivative is the **slope of the tangent line** at a point. A **secant line** connects two points and gives an average slope. As the points move together, that average approaches the derivative.
